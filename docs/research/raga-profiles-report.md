# Sourced grammar reaches thirty of eighty-six ragas

> Written for the first 86 ragas. Since v0.11 the file holds 100 ragas, 73 of them with usable phrases (17 of those only from the repertoire's notation): see `docs/raga-profiles.md`.

The research notes support a usable, source-traced kalpana profile for **30 of the 86 ragas** in Vocal Gym: the concert mainstays among the melakartas (Todi, Mayamalavagowla, Chakravakam, Keeravani, Kharaharapriya, Gourimanohari, Charukesi, Harikambhoji, Shadvidamargini, Pantuvarali, Shanmukhapriya, Hemavati, Dharmavati, Latangi, Vachaspati and Kalyani) and all 14 janyas. Across the 86 ragas the notes yield **488 phrases, 415 of them strong enough to drive generation by default**, plus sourced nyasa lists for 20 ragas, per-swara gamaka notes for 22 (detailed only for Todi, Kalyani, Sankarabharanam, Gourimanohari and Mayamalavagowla), and 40 avoid rules. Three facts shape how a generator should use this. First, several important ragas are nearly empty: **Sankarabharanam and Shubhapantuvarali have no sourced phrases at all**, Natabhairavi and Simhendramadhyamam have only placeholder or Hindustani material, and **47 melakartas have nothing beyond scale, names and composition titles**. Second, the engine's direction rule cannot be broken by any melakarta phrase, because a straight sampurna mela has all seven swaras in both lists; idiom there must come from phrase weights and landing swaras. Third, **13 janya phrases are real idiom that the rule forbids**, such as Malahari's "S R G R S" and Bilahari's anya-swara "P D N2 P D P M G R"; they are kept, flagged and meant to be used only as whole phrases. The profiles are now in the repository as `data/raga-profiles.json`, validated against the engine by `test/raga-profiles.test.js`, with a companion guide in `docs/raga-profiles.md`.

## Thirty ragas carry phrase grammar; forty-seven carry only a scale

Coverage splits sharply by repertoire. The richest material comes from one place: the Darbar "Raga Explorer" pages, which give octave-marked signature phrases together with nyasa and ragachaya swaras, and which supply **241 of the 488 phrases** ([Darbar: Kalyani](https://darbar.org/raga/kalyani/), [Darbar: Mohana](https://darbar.org/raga/mohana/)). Composition notation on shivkumar.org supplies another 115, but its octave dots were lost in extraction, so those phrases carry `octaveCertain: false` and a 20% weight cut ([shivkumar.org: Deva Deva](https://www.shivkumar.org/music/devadeva.htm)). The remaining phrases come from Parrikar, guruguha.org, a pitch study of Todi, a varnam transcription, NIOS and forum practitioners.

The per-raga table with counts, rule exceptions, confidence and landing swaras for all 86 ragas is in `docs/raga-profiles.md` §5. The thin tier is not random: it is the rare melas, and **40 of the 72 melas contain a vivadi pair** (R1–G1, R3–G3, D1–N1 or D3–N3), which Wikipedia says can be "overcome by singing the vivadi swarams in certain phrases" without saying which ([Wikipedia: Vivadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivadi)). Koteeswara Iyer wrote one kriti in every mela specifically to show the vivadi ragas are performable ([Trikala Arts](https://trikalaarts.com/koteeswara-iyer-kritis/)), but only one of those notations was found online ([shivkumar.org: Antaranga bhakti](https://www.shivkumar.org/music/antarangabhakti.htm)).

Two patterns cut across these tables. The sourced nyasa lists rarely match the engine's current default of resting on S and P: Hemavati rests on R and D, Harikambhoji on G, D and N, Latangi on G, P and N. Replacing the "held last swara on Sa or Pa" bonus with each raga's `landingSwaras` is therefore the single change most likely to make generated rounds sound like the raga. And several ragas are defined partly by what they must not do, which the file records as hard or soft avoid entries rather than leaving to chance.

## The direction rule passes every mela phrase but blocks thirteen janya idioms

Every phrase was parsed into pitches with the raga's own swarasthanas and checked step by step with `makeProjector(raga).valid`. For melakartas the check can never fail on a mela swara, because both lists hold all seven swaras; even Kalyani's zig-zag "N G' R' N R' N D N D M D N R'" passes. This means the direction rule gives no idiomatic shape to melakartas at all, and the darbar sancharas confirm that local turn-backs such as "G D P M P G" are normal Carnatic phrasing inside straight ragas.

For the janyas the rule bites, and **13 phrases fail**. Six involve an anya swara the engine leaves out: Bilahari's N2 ([Darbar](https://darbar.org/raga/bilahari/)), Kambhoji's kakali N3 in "S N3. P. D. S" ([Darbar](https://darbar.org/raga/kambhoji/)) and the gitam opening "S' N3 P" ([guruguha.org](https://guruguha.org/the-mystery-about-kambhoji-part-2/)), and three old-Abheri phrases with D1. The other seven step against the arohana: Malahari's "S R G R S" cadence and two related historical motifs ([guruguha.org](https://guruguha.org/?p=1971)), Saveri's "R S R G R R", "D D G'" and "P D N P D" ([Darbar](https://darbar.org/raga/saveri/)), and the modern Abheri phrase "M G G R S R , S", which rises from S to R ([shivkumar.org](https://www.shivkumar.org/music/bhajare-re-manasa.htm)). These are not errors in the sources: Malahari's "G R S" intonation is described as its signature, and Darbar presents Saveri's lines as signature phrases. The file keeps them with `directionRuleOk: false`, and the guidance is to emit them only as atomic whole phrases behind a flag, checking legality at entry and exit, rather than loosening the rule for single steps.

## Exclusions and weights keep weak material out of default use

Each phrase's weight follows one formula: a base of 0.8, 0.6 or 0.25 for high, medium or low source reliability, multiplied by 0.8 when the octave was inferred, plus 0.1 for signature phrases, then capped by context. Hindustani references are capped at 0.15, Dikshitar-school raganga phrases at 0.1 and historical-lakshana forms at 0.3. Overrides are rare and explained in the phrase note: Gajananayutham's phrases sit at 0.5 because the kriti is nominally Vegavahini ([rasikas.org](https://www.rasikas.org/forums/viewtopic.php?p=367947)), the garbled Devar Munivar and Bhajare extractions sit at 0.5 and 0.4, and one Hemavati string with a suspicious P-to-G' leap sits at 0.4.

Some material was **excluded outright**. The Shankar Mahadevan Academy phrase lists for Pantuvarali and Simhendramadhyamam are a template duplicated across both pages ([SMA: Simhendramadhyamam](https://shankarmahadevanacademy.com/raga/simhendramadhyamam/)), so they say nothing about either raga. The same site's lists were also dropped where better data exists (Todi, Mayamalavagowla, Charukesi, Hamsadhwani, Hindolam, Bilahari), since they are mostly scale fragments that the engine's run devices already produce. Wikipedia's "phrase" lists for Sankarabharanam and Harikambhoji were dropped as janya scale patterns ([Wikipedia: Dheerasankarabharanam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dheerasankarabharanam)). Darbar's "kamavardhini" page describes Lalita, an M1 raga, and was excluded ([Darbar](https://darbar.org/raga/kamavardhini/)). Also excluded were the Jalajakshi varnam lines (octaves unassignable), OCR-garbled CBSE fragments, an AI aggregator's Shubhapantuvarali gamaka claims, and Hindustani pakads for ragas with adequate Carnatic data (Vachaspati, Charukesi, Keeravani, Abhogi, Hemavati).

**Down-weighted rather than excluded** are three classes. Dikshitar-school phrases for vivadi melas (Rupavati's SSP form, Kalavati for Yagapriya, Bhogachayanata for Vagadheeswari, Ganasamavarali for Ganamurti) are kept at 0.1. These ragangas were designed to avoid the straight vivadi runs the engine generates: Rupavati's SSP form says "SRGM, PDNS, SNDP and MGRS do not occur" ([guruguha.org](https://guruguha.org/?p=2542)). That rule is recorded as a scale note, not as an avoid rule, because applying it would forbid the sampurna mela's own scale. Two Dikshitar-school sets serve as avoid patterns instead, as the notes recommend: Vegavahini's "S M G M" for Chakravakam, and the Purvikalyani and Gamakakriya markers "P D P S'", "G M D S'" and "D N P D S'" for Gamanashrama ([rasikas.org](https://www.rasikas.org/forums/viewtopic.php?p=354105)). Shanmukhapriya's Raga Nidhi prayogas and Simhendramadhyamam's pakad stay as Hindustani references at 0.15, and Natabhairavi's and Sriranjani's teaching-site phrases stay at 0.2.

## The biggest gaps sit in major ragas and vivadi melas

The most consequential gap is **Sankarabharanam**: it has unusually precise role rules (plain G, nyasa S G M P, N never isolated) but not one notated phrase. Darbar has no page for it, the shivkumar.org notation was rate-limited, and Ragasurabhi's prayogas exist only as audio ([Ragasurabhi](https://ragasurabhi.com/carnatic-music/surabhi-post/post--post0008--myriad-hues-of-ragas-samrat-shankarabaranam.html)). **Shubhapantuvarali** is worse: no phrases, no roles, only mood and naming history ([Sanjay Subrahmanyan](https://www.sanjaysub.com/post/pantuvarali-abheri-etc)). **Natabhairavi** has only near-scalar placeholders and no answer to how it avoids sounding like Bhairavi. **Simhendramadhyamam** has a composer list but no Carnatic phrase ([Chitra Srikrishna site](https://www.chitrasrikrishna.com/post/simhendramadhyamam-raga-ruminations)). **Kharaharapriya** has four phrases with inferred octaves. **Bilahari** has four, and **modern Abheri** has five, all from a garbled extract. Beyond these, the 47 scale-only melas include several with real repertoire, such as Vakulabharanam, Bhavapriya, Rishabhapriya and Vishwambari, whose kritis exist but whose notation was not reached.

The next sources follow from where the gaps are. First is **notation of the Koteeswara Iyer series (Kanda Ganamudam)**, the only body of compositions covering every sampurna mela; one kriti from it already gave Shadvidamargini seven phrases. Next are octave-marked swara passages of specific pieces: "Sami ninne" and "Swararaga sudha" for Sankarabharanam, "Ennalu urake" and Dikshitar's "Sri Satyanarayanam" for Shubhapantuvarali, "Chakkani raja" and "Rama nee samana" from a source that keeps octave marks for Kharaharapriya, "Ninne nammiti" for Simhendramadhyamam, "Marivere" and "Siddhi Vinayakam" for Shanmukhapriya, and "Nagumomu" for modern Abheri. After those come Subbarama Dikshitar's Sangita Sampradaya Pradarsini (for the Dikshitar-school forms and the vivadi question), Sambamoorthy's raga lakshanas (for jiva and nyasa where only inferences exist), the CompMusic/Dunya Saraga annotations, and transcription of the swarakalpana recordings indexed on shivkumar.org, which would give the first direct evidence of kalpana-swara landing practice ([shivkumar.org manodharma index](https://www.shivkumar.org/music/manodharma/index.html)). No source reached documents kalpana-specific conventions, such as eduppu choices or korvai endings, for any raga except Kalyani's general swaraprastara practice ([Parrikar](https://www.parrikar.org/carnatic/kalyani/)).

## Conclusion

The research changes what the kalpana generator's "familiarity" should mean. Today it rewards generic devices and resting on S and P. For the 30 documented ragas, the sources say familiarity is raga-specific: resting on R and D in Hemavati, never holding G in Pantuvarali or Arabhi, sounding P only briefly in Kalyani, and using sourced signature phrases as whole devices. Because the direction rule adds no shape to melakartas, these profile weights are the only idiom the engine can express for them. The file therefore matters most where it is richest, and the thin half should run on generic devices labelled honestly as such, not on borrowed phrases.

The deeper finding is that the missing data is concentrated, not scattered. One composition series and about a dozen named notations would cover most of the gap, including the vivadi melas that no current source explains. Because the schema keeps every phrase traceable, weighted and engine-checked, those additions can go in incrementally: each new notation needs only `--fix` and `npm test` to join the generator safely.
