Vocal Gym. The practice room that knows raga, tala and your voice.

A daily practice system for Carnatic vocalists. It checks how your voice is today, chooses your warm-up, writes exercises that cannot break raga or tala rules, listens for where you leave the raga, and coaches the hardest skill to practise alone: kalpana swaras.

01 / The gap

Serious vocalists practise alone, most of the time, with nothing that listens.

A student meets the guru once or twice a week and practises for hours in between. Mistakes in pitch, rhythm or raga get rehearsed until they stick.

Practice has no structure

Time goes to deciding what to practise. The same exercises repeat while weak ragas and talas stay untouched.

Warm-up is guesswork

A 7 a.m. voice and a 7 p.m. voice after a day of teaching need different warm-ups. Nothing measures which one you have today.

Exercises stop at the textbook

Alankaras are taught in a few ragas and talas. Moving them into Bilahari or Kambhoji, or into Khanda Ata, is left to the student.

Raga errors go unheard

A stray varjya swara or a phrase that belongs to an allied raga passes unnoticed until the next lesson.

Manodharma has no drill

Kalpana swaras must land exactly on the eduppu. Students rarely get structured practice for that.

Dance teachers compose by hand

Jathis must sum to the tala and land on sam. Nattuvanars work the arithmetic out on paper, and a student's jathi waits for the next class to be checked.

02 / What is new

Five things no practice app does today.

High novelty

Generators that cannot break the rules

Alankaras in all 35 talas, starting from the sheet and then new patterns on request; jathis for dancers; kalpana swaras. Temperature is the performer's creativity, from recognisable patterns to unconventional ones, and a feasibility table keeps every output inside raga and tala.

High novelty

Kalpana swara tutor

Rounds built from named devices (runs, janta, dattu, sequences, mora) that must land on the eduppu, a creativity ladder from conventional to adventurous, and a check that the swara you name is the swara you sing.

High novelty

Jathi creator and judge

Generate a jathi whose teermanam lands on sam every time, or write your own in JathiScript. The judge checks the arithmetic exactly, scores six aspects of craft and tells you which line to fix.

Medium-high novelty

Raga conformance coach

Not "which raga is this?" but "where did you leave the raga you chose, and towards which allied raga?", phrase by phrase.

High novelty

Personal voice model

A 90-second daily Voice Check on the phone, compared with your own rested baseline, chooses the warm-up and learns which warm-ups actually help you.

Table stakes

The practice shell

Session timer, tanpura, all 35 talas, logs and streaks. Necessary, familiar, and built first.

03 / A session, in order

One flow from "how is my voice today" to "what did I learn".

  1. Session start

    Time of day, vocal load since waking, minutes available.

  2. Voice Check

    90 seconds sung to the drone: sustained Sa, soft high onsets, a siren, Sarali 1.

  3. Strain estimate

    Today against your rested baseline, with a confidence band.

  4. Warm-up chosen

    Inside voice-science limits: strained voices get semi-occluded work only.

  5. Warm-up and re-check

    Timed exercises; a short re-check tells the model whether they helped.

  6. Pattern practice

    The 35 tala alankaras in your raga, then new patterns, with live intonation feedback.

  7. Repertoire

    Spaced repetition picks compositions; the coach marks phrases that drift.

  8. Manodharma

    Kalpana swara rounds that must land on the eduppu.

  9. Summary

    Minutes by phase, streaks, teacher view; the personal model updates overnight.

04 / Generators

Creativity on a dial. Rules that never bend.

One engine encodes the tala (angas, jati, nadai, eduppu) and the raga (which swaras may be used going up and coming down). It builds a table of every state from which a valid ending is still possible, then samples only from those. Temperature is the performer's creativity: low keeps to patterns listeners recognise, high reaches for unconventional ones. It can never produce a wrong one.

Alankaras across 35 talas and every raga class

  • The first pattern in each of the 35 talas is the line from the 35 tala alankara sheet, reproduced note for note: all 35 first lines up and down, and every line of Chaturashra Dhruva.
  • "New pattern" gives one the student has not seen: it differs from the sheet and from every earlier variation for that raga and tala. At low temperature it keeps the sheet's idiom; at high temperature it reaches for leaps, turns and syncopation.
  • Audava, shadava and sampurna ragas, including janyas whose ascent and descent differ (Bilahari, Malahari, Kambhoji): line counts adapt, and the few swaras that would break a rule are corrected and underlined.
  • Tested on 86 ragas × 35 talas: 23,992 lines, every step valid; 0.30% of swaras needed correction, all in asymmetric janyas.

Jathi creator, guide and judge

Students write jathis in JathiScript, where one word is one matra. A worked example in Adi, two avartanams from sam:

let A = tha-ka-dhi-mi · let B = tha-ka-jho-nu
$A $B $A $B | tha , dhi , $A | tha-ka-tha-ki-ta tha-ki-ta ||
tha-ka-tha-ki-ta ka-tha-ki-ta tha-ki-ta {tha-dhi-gi-na-thom}/3
  • Gate: 65 slots, the final "thom" on sam, every bar on an anga line
  • Judge: 100 / 100, with a gopuccha yati 5 → 4 → 3 and teermanam statements on beats
  • Delete one karvai and the judge answers: "Start with line 11: the phrases before the bar there are 1 matra short."

Kalpana swara tutor

Temperature is the singer's creativity. The same slot in Kalyani, Adi, second speed, landing on Pa:

T 0 · S R G M P M , D N P · D N P · D N P → eduppu
T 2 · S , Ṇ M P , R G M Ṡ Ṗ Ṁ , Ṙ D P → eduppu

At 0 every swara comes from conventional devices (a run, a held swara, a mora); at 2, 44% come from unconventional ones (wide leaps, a leap that climbs back, a rise into the upper octave). Both obey Kalyani's direction rules and land exactly. The tutor then listens: landing error, out-of-raga swaras, devices used, and whether the swara you named matches the pitch you sang.

05 / Listening

A coach asks where you left the raga, not which raga you sang.

Raga recognition is a crowded research area with closed-set accuracies around 95%. A learner has already chosen the raga. What helps is knowing which phrase left it, how, and which allied raga it drifted towards.

Clean signal

The app synthesises its own tanpura, so it can subtract that exact signal from the microphone before tracking pitch. The tonic is known, not guessed.

Swara tokens with gamaka

Carnatic-tuned pitch tracking, then segmentation into held notes and turning points, the units Carnatic intonation research measures.

Three scorers

Swara placement against the raga's own intonation; transitions against the raga grammar (varjya, vakra); phrases against allied ragas such as Kalyani and Yamuna Kalyani, or Darbar and Nayaki.

Mistakes made on purpose

Valid phrases are altered in controlled ways (a forbidden swara, an allied-raga phrase, a flattened gamaka) to create labelled training data without thousands of hours of annotation.

Your teacher's ear

Teachers set different tolerances; one study found 12 cents for one teacher and about 22 for another. Each student inherits their teacher's profile.

06 / Voice

A warm-up chosen for the voice you have today.

What research shows

  • Fatigue shows up differently in different people, so models need personal baselines.
  • Neural speech embeddings separated rested from fatigued speech at 81–85% for one lecturer, falling to 70–76% for a new speaker without adaptation.
  • Wearables can measure vocal dose and alert singers, but need hardware and measure load, not condition.
  • Evidence on warm-ups is positive but mixed and drawn from small studies, so each singer's response is worth measuring.

What Vocal Gym does

  • A standard 90-second Voice Check sung to the app's drone, every day, on the phone.
  • Voice-quality features and self-supervised embeddings, normalised per recording and compared with your own rested days.
  • A confidence band, so the app says when it is unsure.
  • A warm-up chooser that learns from before-and-after checks, inside fixed safety rules.
  • Wellness feedback, never a diagnosis. Hoarseness that lasts for weeks gets a referral to an ENT or speech-language pathologist.

Live one-to-one classes: FY25 operating income ₹27.74 crore, net loss ₹20.30 crore, ₹19.2 crore Series A. Live teaching sells but costs a lot to deliver.

Learner base

09 / Roadmap

Useful in month four. Intelligent by month eighteen.

Months 0–4

Practice core and engine

  • Session flow, drone, 35 talas
  • 35 sheet alankaras and new patterns; jathi creator and judge; kalpana generator
  • Voice Check recordings begin (data only)
  • Teacher panel of 5–8
Months 4–10

Listening

  • On-device pitch and drone cancellation
  • Live intonation; raga conformance v1
  • Kalpana tutor levels 1–3
  • Strain pilot: about 30 singers, 8 weeks
Months 10–18

Personalisation

  • Strain model with confidence bands
  • Adaptive warm-ups; n-of-1 trials
  • Teacher dashboard; jathi studio
  • Papers and dataset release
Months 18–24

Expansion

  • Hindustani packs
  • Voice Check for every singer
  • Institutions and choirs

Main risks

  • A small pure-Carnatic market
  • Hobbyist churn and low conversion
  • Gurus' trust and differences between schools
  • Health claims and voice-data privacy

How we handle them

  • Grow through the voice layer, Hindustani and dance
  • Teacher-assigned routines and B2B seats
  • Teacher overrides and bani-specific tolerances
  • Wellness framing; on-device processing; DPDP Act 2023 compliance
